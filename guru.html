01

Where It All Began

Guru Neha Muthiyan received her training in Kathak under the guidance of Guru Pandita Shambhavi Dandekar, whose mentorship has played an important role in shaping her artistic journey. She began her teaching career at Guru Pandita Manisha Tai Sathe's institute, where she gained valuable experience as a Kathak teacher.

In 2008, she founded Kathak Pathshala with the aim of providing authentic training in the Banaras Gharana tradition of Kathak. As the Founder and Director, she continues to guide students through structured training while encouraging a deeper understanding and appreciation of Indian classical dance.

02

The Many Roles of a Guru

Along with teaching, Guru Neha Muthiyan has contributed to the field of dance through a number of educational and cultural initiatives.

In 2014, she founded Loud Applause, a dance magazine created to help dancers and enthusiasts explore the history, traditions, and different styles of dance. The magazine featured articles by renowned artists and scholars, making dance knowledge accessible to a wider audience. Today, Loud Applause continues as an online platform.

In 2024, she served as the Guest Editor for the Pune Special Edition of Attendance, a national-level annual publication dedicated to dance, marking its 25th year. Working closely with renowned dance critic, scholar, and historian Shri Ashish Khokar, she contributed to documenting Pune's dance journey from 1947 onwards. The edition highlights the city's leading dance institutions, gurus, distinguished dancers, and technical aspects of dance, while also featuring insightful articles and words of wisdom from Guru Manisha Tai Sathe, Guru Shama Bhate, and Guru Sucheta Sathe. This special edition stands as a valuable record of Pune's rich dance heritage.

She has also contributed to Balbharati by writing educational content that introduces school students to the fundamentals of dance and its cultural significance.

03

In the Service of the Art

Guru Neha Muthiyan is one of the four founding members of the Pune International Dance Festival (PIDF). Started by a group of Nrutya Sakhis, the festival was created to provide a platform for emerging artists from different dance traditions. Over the years, it has grown into an annual celebration that brings together artists and audiences through Indian dance.

She has also curated and contributed to several dance initiatives that encourage artistic exploration and learning, including:

  • The Poetry Festival – Exploring Vachik Abhinaya
  • Pravahee – Thematic Compositions
  • Sanchari – Films on Dance

She has also been associated with SPIC MACAY, conducting lecture-demonstrations in Zilla Parishad schools across Pune. Through these sessions, she introduces students to Indian classical dance, helping young learners experience and appreciate the art form.
